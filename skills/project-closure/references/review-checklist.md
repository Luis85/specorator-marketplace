# Review checklist — closure, lessons, handover, benefits review

✅ pass / ⚠️ weak / ❌ blocking. The test that exposes most gaps: *what would the organisation do
differently on the next project because of this report?*

## Closure report — honesty

- [ ] Outcome stated plainly in the opening paragraph, including failure or premature stop.
- [ ] Every original success criterion assessed with evidence, not narrative.
- [ ] Performance reported against the **original** baseline as well as the current one.
- [ ] Change volume shown as a percentage of the original budget.
- [ ] Contingency consumption reported.
- [ ] No re-framing of missed objectives as "delivered differently".

## Acceptance

- [ ] Acceptance recorded per deliverable with an accepting authority and a date.
- [ ] Conditional acceptances state their conditions and who tracks them.
- [ ] Refused or withheld acceptance is recorded, with the consequence.

## Outstanding items

- [ ] Every open defect, deferred requirement, open risk, and open action is listed.
- [ ] Every item has a **destination**: named owner plus vehicle (ops backlog, follow-on project,
      formally accepted closed-unresolved).
- [ ] Items accepted as closed-unresolved name who accepted them.
- [ ] Nothing is left simply "open".
- [ ] Severity is stated so operations know what they are inheriting.

## Benefits

- [ ] Realised-to-date is distinguished from forecast; the original forecast is not presented as
      an outcome.
- [ ] Each benefit has a named owner in the receiving business who has accepted it.
- [ ] Measurement mechanism named: measure, source, collector, and reporting destination.
- [ ] A review date is set, with an owner, and it sits in a governance forum's agenda.
- [ ] Benefits now unachievable are stated with the reason.
- [ ] Business changes still required for benefits to appear are named and owned.

## Lessons

- [ ] Drawn from a log maintained during delivery, not invented at the end.
- [ ] Each lesson has context, event, quantified effect, root cause, recommendation, and owner.
- [ ] Each recommendation names the artifact that changes (standard, template, rate, clause,
      checklist, training).
- [ ] Owners control the thing that must change; none are "the project team".
- [ ] What worked is captured, not only what failed.
- [ ] Root causes point at conditions and systems, not individuals.
- [ ] Reference-class data recorded: planned vs actual duration, cost, effort, change volume.
- [ ] Lessons have been sent to their owners, not just filed.

## Handover

- [ ] Operational readiness assessed against explicit criteria, not a general assurance.
- [ ] Support model, runbooks, monitoring, backup/recovery, and access transfer all covered.
- [ ] Known defects handed over with workarounds.
- [ ] Training delivered and competence confirmed, not just scheduled.
- [ ] Hypercare has a start, an end, staffing, and an exit test.
- [ ] Operations have formally accepted, and anything refused has a remediation plan.
- [ ] Third-party support and warranty arrangements are in force and known to operations.

## Administrative closure

- [ ] Contracts closed, final invoices settled, retentions released.
- [ ] Licences and access transferred or terminated.
- [ ] Data retention and deletion obligations discharged.
- [ ] Superseded assets decommissioned, or the decommissioning owned by someone named.
- [ ] Records archived with a stated location.
- [ ] Financial account closed; team formally released.

## Post-implementation review (when reviewing that artifact)

- [ ] Held on the scheduled date, with the benefit owners present.
- [ ] Actual vs target per benefit, with the reason for each variance.
- [ ] Recoverable shortfall separated from permanent shortfall, with actions and owners.
- [ ] A verdict on whether the investment was worth it, against the original appraisal.
- [ ] Learning fed back into the appraisal and estimating process, not only into project practice.
- [ ] Unwelcome findings stated plainly.

## Common findings, phrased usefully

| Finding | Say it like this |
|---|---|
| Baseline laundering | "Performance is measured against the June re-baseline only. Against the approved baseline the project was 11 weeks and €240k over — report both." |
| Homeless open items | "Fourteen open defects are listed with no owner. Assign each to the ops backlog with a named owner, or have the senior user accept them as closed-unresolved." |
| Lessons as observations | "'Better stakeholder engagement was needed' changes nothing. Name the artifact to change — add operations to the mandatory stakeholder list in the planning template — and give it to the PMO lead." |
| Unowned benefits | "No benefit owner is named after handover and no review date is set. Nominate the operations director for B1–B3 and book the 6-month review now, before the team disbands." |
| Forecast as outcome | "§5 reports the €1.2m benefit as achieved; nothing has yet been measured. Report realised-to-date as zero with the measurement starting in month 3." |
| Unsigned handover | "The handover checklist has no operations signature and three unmet criteria. Do not close until either signed or the gaps are accepted with a remediation owner." |
| Quiet benefit disposal | "Two benefits from the business case do not appear at all. State them and their status — including 'no longer achievable, because …'." |
| No reference data | "Planned-vs-actual ratios are not recorded. Add them; they are the most reusable output of this closure for future estimating." |
