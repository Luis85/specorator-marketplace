# Review checklist — project brief

✅ pass / ⚠️ weak / ❌ blocking. A brief with any ❌ should not go to a decision body.

## Purpose

- [ ] The decision being requested is stated in the first few lines, with a date.
- [ ] The cost of the *next step* is stated, not just the whole project.
- [ ] The brief supports one decision; it has not become a plan or a full business case.
- [ ] Four pages or fewer.

## Problem and justification

- [ ] The problem is described with evidence, not asserted.
- [ ] Consequence of inaction is concrete (cost, risk, deadline, missed benefit).
- [ ] Do-nothing appears as a real option with its consequences.
- [ ] At least one alternative option is named and dismissed for a stated reason.
- [ ] The strategic link is real — a named objective, obligation, or portfolio goal.

## Objectives

- [ ] Three to five objectives, no more.
- [ ] Each is specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, and time-bound.
- [ ] Each could be judged pass or fail at closure without further interpretation.
- [ ] Objectives describe outcomes, not activities ("claims processed in under 2 days",
      not "implement a claims system").
- [ ] No objective silently depends on work that is out of scope.

## Scope

- [ ] An out-of-scope list exists and is specific.
- [ ] Interfaces to adjacent work or other projects are named.
- [ ] Products are nouns with a recipient, not verbs.
- [ ] Nothing in the objectives lies outside the stated scope.

## Numbers

- [ ] Cost and duration are ranges with a stated basis and confidence.
- [ ] Benefits have a measure, a baseline, a target, and a date — or are marked TBD with an owner.
- [ ] No number appears whose source cannot be identified.
- [ ] Funding source is named or flagged as unresolved.

## Risk, assumptions, dependencies

- [ ] Five risks at most, each written as cause → event → effect.
- [ ] Each risk states its effect on a specific objective.
- [ ] Assumptions have an owner and a validate-by date.
- [ ] Dependencies name the other party.

## Governance

- [ ] A single accountable sponsor is named (a person, not a committee).
- [ ] The decision body and its authority are named.
- [ ] The proposed next step names its artifacts and duration.

## Clarity

- [ ] No unexplained acronyms or method jargon.
- [ ] Readable by someone who has never heard of the project.
- [ ] Open questions are listed rather than papered over.

## Common findings, phrased usefully

| Finding | Say it like this |
|---|---|
| Unmeasurable objective | "§3.2 'improve customer satisfaction' has no measure or target. Give it a metric, a baseline, and a date, or move it to the benefits section as an aspiration." |
| Missing out-of-scope | "There is no out-of-scope list. Add the three adjacent asks most likely to arrive later — mobile app, legacy data before 2019, and the partner portal — with a yes/no." |
| Benefit with no baseline | "§6 claims €1.2m annual saving with no current-state figure. State today's cost and the measurement method, or mark it TBD with an owner." |
| Brief as plan | "§5 lists 40 tasks with dates. Cut to named outputs; the plan belongs after initiation is authorised." |
| Committee as sponsor | "The sponsor is 'the steering group'. Name the individual accountable for the business case." |
