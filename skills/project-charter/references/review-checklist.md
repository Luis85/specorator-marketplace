# Review checklist — project charter / PID

✅ pass / ⚠️ weak / ❌ blocking. A charter that cannot settle a future argument is not done.

## Authority

- [ ] A single accountable sponsor is named, with the capacity to commit the budget.
- [ ] The project manager is named and their delegated authority is quantified.
- [ ] Accountability and authority match — nobody is accountable for something they cannot decide.
- [ ] The document is signed, dated, and versioned, and it says what it supersedes.
- [ ] The period or stage being authorised is explicit.

## Objectives and success

- [ ] Objectives and success criteria are separate things, both present.
- [ ] Each success criterion has a measure, a target, an assessor, and a date.
- [ ] At least one criterion is assessed after go-live, not only at handover.
- [ ] Criteria are consistent with the business case's benefits, not a different story.

## Scope

- [ ] Out-of-scope list is present and specific.
- [ ] Interfaces name the other party, the exchange, and the date.
- [ ] Scope is consistent with the brief and the business case; differences are deliberate and noted.

## Deliverables and acceptance

- [ ] Every deliverable has a named acceptance authority (a person or role, not "the business").
- [ ] Acceptance criteria exist or are referenced in a named artifact.
- [ ] Deliverables are outputs, not activities.

## Milestones and governance

- [ ] Each milestone carries a decision and the evidence needed for it.
- [ ] Board composition covers business, user, and supplier interests.
- [ ] Decision rights per role are stated, not implied.
- [ ] Escalation route includes an expected response time.
- [ ] Reporting cadence lines up with board cadence.

## Tolerances

- [ ] All six dimensions have a tolerance or an explicit "none".
- [ ] Tolerances are numeric where they can be.
- [ ] Each has a trigger and an escalation target.
- [ ] Contingency ownership and release mechanism are stated.

## Management approaches

- [ ] Change control names who decides what, at which threshold.
- [ ] Risk approach names the register owner and review frequency.
- [ ] Quality/acceptance approach names who tests and who accepts.
- [ ] Communication approach names the audiences and the cadence.
- [ ] Where an organisational standard is adopted, it is cited by name and version.
- [ ] Where the project delivers or uses AI, human oversight and compliance obligations are addressed.

## Discipline

- [ ] No large sections copied from the business case or plan — references instead.
- [ ] No task-level schedule inside the charter.
- [ ] Assumptions/constraints/dependencies are summarised, with the RAID log as the source of truth.
- [ ] Length is proportionate: a handful of pages for most projects.

## Common findings, phrased usefully

| Finding | Say it like this |
|---|---|
| No tolerances | "§8 is empty, so every variance is either an escalation or invisible. Set at least cost ±10% and time ±2 weeks with the board as escalation target." |
| Unassessable criterion | "'Stakeholders are satisfied' has no measure. Use the post-go-live survey score with a target, and name who reads it." |
| Authority gap | "The PM is accountable for the €800k budget but may commit only €2k. Raise the commitment limit or move the accountability to the sponsor." |
| Unowned acceptance | "D3 has no acceptance authority. Name the person who can reject it, or it will be accepted by silence." |
| Charter as plan | "§5 contains a 60-line task schedule that will be stale in a fortnight. Keep the six stage-boundary milestones and reference the schedule artifact." |
| Story drift | "Success criteria promise 30% faster processing; the business case claims 15%. Reconcile before sign-off." |
